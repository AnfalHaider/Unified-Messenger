Unified Messenger
Privacy

Every customer waiting for a reply, in one line.

Unified Messenger is a Windows app for a business with several WhatsApp, Instagram and Google accounts. It watches the accounts you are already signed in to and shows who has been waiting, for how long, and at which branch. It reads; you reply.

In development. It runs on the business's own PCs, and there is nothing to sign up for to use it.

What it does

  • One line, worst first

    Every unanswered conversation across every account, longest wait at the top, with the reply target you set and a warning before it runs out.

  • Your own accounts

    Each account stays signed in on the PC, in its own window, exactly as you would use it in a browser. The app never sends a message, replies to a review, or clicks anything in a chat.

  • Reply times and backlog

    How quickly the business answers, which branch is behind, what was missed yesterday, and Google reviews that still have no reply.

  • An assistant on the PC

    Optional, off until you switch it on, and it runs on the machine itself. It answers from the app's own figures and can draft a reply for you to send yourself.

Where the information lives

Messages, customer names, reply times and the account logins stay on the PC that read them. They are never uploaded, and there is no analytics or crash reporting of any kind.

Signing in with Google is only for workspaces: it lets a business share its setup — the list of accounts, locations, opening hours, reply targets and saved replies — between its own PCs. That, your name and your email address are the only things the app ever sends off the machine. The privacy policy sets out exactly what is kept and where.
